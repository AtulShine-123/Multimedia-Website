LOG_01 WHY THESE PIECES? +
Black and White Synthwave: I chose this piece because I really wanted to experiment with negative space and high contrast. The Synthwave aesthetic is usually very colorful, so stripping it down to just black and white forced me to rely entirely on line and shape to make the image recognizable. It represents my interest in retro-futurism but with a minimalist twist.

The Owl: This vector illustration was chosen because it shows my progress with creating organic forms using digital tools. I wanted to focus on clean lines and distinct blocks of color rather than blending. It stands out in my portfolio because it feels like a professional logo or sticker design, which is a style I enjoy creating.

The Porsche GT3 RS: I selected this car illustration because it was my most ambitious project regarding detail and color. I love automotive design, and I wanted to capture the aggressive shape of the GT3 RS. It contrasts well with my other two pieces because it uses a vibrant, multi-colored background and complex mechanical lines compared to the simpler nature scenes.
LOG_02 CREATIVE PROCESS? +
Black and White Synthwave: My favorite part of creating this was working on the perspective grid. Getting the lines to converge towards the "sun" created a strong sense of depth and movement. It was satisfying to see a flat image suddenly look like a 3D landscape just by manipulating the lines.

The Owl: The best part of this process was designing the shadow and highlight shapes on the feathers. Instead of using a gradient tool, I had to manually draw the jagged shapes to imply texture. It was fun figuring out exactly where the light source (the moon) was hitting the bird and how that changed the value of the brown feathers.

The Porsche GT3 RS: I really enjoyed coloring this piece, specifically the background stripes. Matching the rainbow background to the race car livery was fun because it made the whole image pop. I also liked drawing the wheels; even though they were difficult, getting the geometric pattern of the spokes right felt like solving a puzzle.
LOG_03 FUTURE IMPROVEMENTS? +
Black and White Synthwave: If I were to improve this, I would add more texture to the palm trees. Right now, they are solid white silhouettes, but adding some small black cuts or lines inside the leaves would give them more definition and make them look less flat against the grid.

The Owl: I would change the background to add more atmosphere. While the blue box works as a frame, adding a subtle gradient or maybe some clouds behind the moon would create more emphasis on the owl. I also think the claws on the branch could be defined better to make the bird look like it's gripping the wood tighter.

The Porsche GT3 RS: I would work on the value and shading on the car's body. The colors are accurate, but the car looks a little flat. Adding darker shadows underneath the spoiler and lighter highlights on the hood would give the car more form and make it look 3D, rather than just a sticker on a background. I’d also clean up the "GT3 RS" text in the back to make the lines smoother.

UNIT INTRODUCTION

Cultural art is a window into the values, beliefs, and histories of different communities around the world. Through paintings, sculptures, music, and performances, people express their identity and traditions in unique ways. Studying cultural art helps us understand how societies communicate ideas and preserve their heritage. It also shows the influence of environment, religion, and social customs on artistic expression. By exploring these works, we can appreciate both the diversity and the common threads that connect human creativity.

◈

UNIT REFLECTION

During this Cultural Art Unit, I learned a lot about how art reflects the history and identity of different communities. In my videos, I talked about how exploring figures like MLK and Bob Marley helped me understand the power of art in spreading messages of change and unity. I also reflected on my redesigned flags and identity infographic, which made me think about how personal and cultural symbols can tell a story about who we are. Creating these projects challenged me to connect historical ideas with my own perspective and experiences. I realized that cultural art isn’t just about aesthetics, it’s a way to communicate, remember, and inspire. Overall, this unit helped me appreciate both the diversity of cultures and the personal impact of creative expression.

TOP 3 DESIGNS

TOP_01 WATCH // PRODUCT DESIGN REFLECTION +
Looking at this presentation, I can see a clear evolution in my modeling skills, transitioning from the boxy, rigid geometry of the blue and grey iterations to the highly refined, market-ready look of the circular Design 3. The product breakdown on the left is exceptionally strong; including clear dimensions, a features list, and orthographic views shows that I am thinking like a professional product designer rather than just a 3D hobbyist. However, my execution on the poster's typography and branding needs work. Using a default sans-serif font across the center canvas makes the presentation feel a bit unpolished, and while the self-aware "Samsang" text injects some humor, it also highlights that the core design is heavily reliant on an existing brand's aesthetic. To level this up, I need to focus on cleaner typographic hierarchy and push myself to develop a completely unique design language that doesn't rely on a corporate parody.
TOP_02 GT3RS // MY CREATIVE COLOR WHEEL +
My Creative Color Wheel (Porsche GT3RS). Looking at this piece, I feel I nailed the vector illustration of the Porsche. It captures that aggressive, track-focused stance and the body lines really well. However, I have to admit the background and typography are fighting the car for attention. Those intensely saturated diagonal rainbow stripes flatten the depth and pull my eyes away from the main subject. Also, the large "GT3RS" text in the background has uneven, organic line weights that clash with the precise, mechanical feel of the car. To improve this, I need to either tighten up that typography to match the car's geometry or mute the background colors so the vehicle can truly pop as the focal point.
TOP_03 CULTURAL REDESIGNED FLAGS // ART UNIT +
My Cultural Redesigned Flags. I'm proud of the concepts behind these redesigns, especially the ambitious fusion of national identities. The star-shaped emblem does a good job of weaving the Indian Ashoka Chakra with the US stars and stripes, though I can see now that the curving lines at the tips feel slightly cramped by the star's geometry. The circular landscape emblem is probably my strongest technical work here—the smooth gradients, clean silhouettes, and dynamic waves came out beautifully. My biggest critique of my own work, though, is the presentation layout. I introduced three original flags at the top but only provided two redesigned emblems at the bottom. It creates a conceptual mismatch, and a viewer is going to struggle to map the three original flags directly to my two new designs. I really need to adjust this layout to explicitly show which elements combined into which final emblem to improve the storytelling of my presentation.
